The 30-Second Mental Model

— Why SSDs suddenly slow down or spike latency
The Core Problem

RAM lets you overwrite any byte in 80 nanoseconds forever. 3D NAND flash packs 100× more capacity per dollar, but comes with weird physical rules that create sudden speed cliffs.

Plain-English Analogy

Imagine writing in pen on a notebook where you can only erase an entire chapter at once: when pages fill up, you must copy good paragraphs to a fresh chapter before erasing the old one (Garbage Collection).

Why You Care at SanDisk

Nearly every mysterious throughput drop or 10 ms tail-latency spike you debug on SN861, UltraQLC, or SN8100 comes from either Garbage Collection or LDPC error retries.

Know Your SanDisk Hardware Lineup (2025–2026)

— Plain-English guide to BiCS8/9/10 & shipping SSDs

SanDisk (in its joint venture with Kioxia) builds 3D NAND using CBA (CMOS directly Bonded to Array): peripheral logic is made on one wafer for max speed, 3D flash layers on another wafer for max density, then bonded face-to-face.

NAND Generations

BiCS8 → BiCS9 → BiCS10

  • BiCS8 (218 layers): Today’s mass-production workhorse (3.6 Gb/s I/O, ~42 μs read).
  • BiCS9 (2 Tb QLC): Pairs proven BiCS8 cells with faster 4.8 Gb/s CMOS for higher QLC speed & power efficiency (vendor-reported).
  • BiCS10 (332 layers): Next-gen 4.8 Gb/s Toggle DDR6.0 + low-voltage PI-LTT to saturate PCIe 6.0 SSDs (sampling 2026).
Enterprise & AI Lakes

SN861 & 256TB UltraQLC

  • SN861 (Gen5 TLC, up to 16TB): OCP-inspired low-latency workhorse for AI training & cloud DBs.
  • SN670 (128TB) & UltraQLC (256TB “Stargate”): Writes directly to QLC without an SLC buffer (PLP-safe!) for flat steady-state speed + Dynamic Frequency Scaling (DFS). Validation catch: filling 256TB takes huge wall-clock time!
Client & Gaming

WD_BLACK SN8100 / Optimus

  • M.2 PCIe 5.0 x4 (218L BiCS8 TLC) hitting 14.9 GB/s read & 2.3M+ IOPS (independently verified in reviews) at just 6.5–7.0W active / 5 mW idle.
  • Uses nCache 4.0 dynamic SLC caching — validate burst exhaustion/recovery and DirectStorage gaming latency.
TLC vs. QLC

TLC stores 3 bits/cell (8 voltage states — faster, higher endurance); QLC stores 4 bits/cell (16 tight voltage states — ultra-dense for AI data lakes).

FTL & DRAM vs. HMB

Flash Translation Layer maps logical sectors to physical flash pages. Enterprise SSDs keep this huge map in onboard DRAM; DRAM-less client SSDs borrow a slice of host PC RAM (HMB).
